The problem

Open almost any standard English Bible to Exodus 3:14: God tells Moses His name — and then translators spend the rest of the Old Testament replacing it with LORD and GOD in small caps. Four hundred years of tradition, and you can read the Bible cover to cover without once encountering the name God gave Himself.

Messianic translations are all responses to that problem, plus one more: the New Testament was written by Jews, about a Jewish Messiah, quoting a Hebrew Bible. The four things they change, in some combination:

Names

Jesus → Yeshua. Christ → Messiah/Moshiach. God → Elohim/Elōhīm. Mary → Miriam. James → Ya'akov.

The Name

What to do with YHWH: LORD, ADONAI, Yehovah, Yahweh, or the Hebrew letters themselves.

Book order

Some restore the Tanakh order — Torah, Nevi'im (Prophets), Ketuvim (Writings) — instead of the Christian Old Testament order.

Reading level and lens

Sentence-by-sentence from Hebrew/Greek, paraphrase for readability — or, in the NMV's case, bracketed identifications showing who is speaking.

No translation is neutral. Every one makes choices. This guide shows you the choices, so you can pick the one whose choices match what you want.

How the guide helps you choose

  1. 1. Start with the question

    Study (CJB + commentary, or the NMV's bracketed identifications)? Read aloud with family (TLV)? See the Messiah in the Tanakh (NMV)? The guide matches your goal to the translation.

  2. 2. Try before you buy

    The NMV's complete text is free on YouVersion. Read Psalm 23 and Isaiah 7 in all three before spending anything.

  3. 3. Know what you're buying

    A fresh translation (NMV, TLV) and a paraphrase of a 1917 version (the CJB's Tanakh) are different kinds of work. Neither is automatically better — but you should know which you are buying.
